# StayKnit — Trademarking To-Do List

**Owner:** StayKnit (Pty) Ltd · CIPC reg. 2026/740258/07 · Paternoster, Western Cape, South Africa
**Purpose:** A practical, ordered checklist to secure trademark protection for the **StayKnit** name, logo, and related marks.
**Status:** Draft working list — tick items as you go.

> **Not legal advice.** Trademark filing has hard legal consequences (priority dates, class scope, oppositions). Use this list to get organised and to brief a South African trademark attorney; let counsel confirm classes, wording, and strategy before you file. Dates, fees, and registry rules must be verified with CIPC / your attorney at the time of filing.

---

## Phase 0 — Decide what you are protecting

- [ ] List every mark you want to protect, in priority order:
  - [ ] **Word mark** "StayKnit" (most important — protects the name in any font/styling).
  - [ ] **Logo / device mark** (the StayKnit logo as used in the app and marketing).
  - [ ] **Combined mark** (word + logo lock-up), if the styled lock-up is core to the brand.
  - [ ] Any **tagline/slogan** you rely on (e.g. the current hero line) — only if it is distinctive and you intend to keep it.
- [ ] Confirm the exact spelling, casing, and spacing of the name as you want it registered ("StayKnit").
- [ ] Gather clean source assets: high-resolution logo (SVG + PNG), colour references, and the word mark in plain text.
- [ ] Decide territories:
  - [ ] **South Africa** (home market — first priority, filed at CIPC).
  - [ ] Any other countries where you have or plan real business/users. **Note:** South Africa is **not** a member of the Madrid Protocol, so international protection generally means separate national filings (or regional routes) — confirm the current position and cost with counsel before committing.

## Phase 1 — Clearance / knock-out search (do this BEFORE filing)

- [ ] Run a preliminary **CIPC trade mark search** for "StayKnit" and close variants (StayKnit, Stay Knit, StayKnitt, phonetic equivalents).
- [ ] Search common-law use: Google, app stores, domain registrars, social handles, Companies register.
- [ ] Check for conflicts specifically in the classes you intend to file (see Phase 2).
- [ ] Commission a **professional availability search** through a trademark attorney (their search is far more reliable than a self-search and flags risky similar marks).
- [ ] Record the outcome: is "StayKnit" clear to register, or does counsel recommend a variation?
- [ ] If a serious conflict exists, decide early: adjust the mark, narrow classes, or take advice on coexistence.

## Phase 2 — Choose the classes (Nice Classification)

> Trademarks are registered per class of goods/services. A SaaS/property-management product like StayKnit typically spans software + services. Confirm the final list with counsel.

- [ ] **Class 42** — Software as a Service (SaaS); design and development of computer software; hosting; providing online non-downloadable software for short-stay/rental management and calendar synchronisation. *(Primary class for StayKnit.)*
- [ ] **Class 9** — Downloadable software / mobile applications (if you distribute an installable app or PWA that counts as downloadable).
- [ ] **Class 35** — Business management/administration services; commercial administration; advertising — relevant to the channel-management, bookings-admin, and owner-statement side of the product. *(Confirm relevance with counsel.)*
- [ ] Consider **Class 36** only if you position anything as financial/payment services (you use Paystack as the processor, so this may be unnecessary — check).
- [ ] For each chosen class, draft a **specification** (the list of goods/services) that matches what StayKnit actually does and plans to do — broad enough to be useful, specific enough to survive examination.

## Phase 3 — Engage an attorney & prepare the application

- [ ] Appoint a South African trademark attorney (or patent-and-trademark firm). Get a fixed-fee quote per mark, per class.
- [ ] Provide them: the mark(s), the asset files, the class list + specifications, and the applicant details below.
- [ ] Confirm the **applicant of record** is the company, not you personally: **StayKnit (Pty) Ltd**, reg. 2026/740258/07, registered address in Paternoster, Western Cape. (Owning marks in the company keeps brand assets with the business.)
- [ ] Decide filing order/strategy with counsel (e.g. word mark first, logo second; SA first, foreign later).
- [ ] Approve the draft specification and representation of each mark before submission.

## Phase 4 — File

- [ ] File the **word mark "StayKnit"** in the agreed classes at CIPC.
- [ ] File the **logo/device mark** (and combined mark if used).
- [ ] Record each application's **filing date and application number** (the filing date sets your priority — keep it safe).
- [ ] Diarise the expected examination timeline given by counsel.
- [ ] If filing abroad, file national applications in the target countries within any priority window counsel advises (a Convention priority claim may let foreign filings back-date to the SA filing if made in time — verify the window).

## Phase 5 — Prosecution (examination → advertisement → registration)

- [ ] Track the examination. Respond to any **CIPC examiner objections / official actions** within the deadlines (counsel handles the wording).
- [ ] On acceptance, the mark is **advertised in the Patent Journal** for opposition. Diarise the opposition window.
- [ ] Watch for any **third-party opposition**; instruct counsel to respond if one is filed.
- [ ] On successful completion, obtain the **registration certificate** and file it with your company records.
- [ ] Note that registration can take a while end-to-end — treat it as a background process and keep the diary current.

## Phase 6 — After registration: use, mark, and maintain

- [ ] Once registered, use the **®** symbol with the registered mark in the registered territory/classes. Until then, use **™** for an unregistered mark you are claiming — do **not** use ® before registration.
- [ ] Keep the copyright/footer line separate from trademark notices (the site footer already auto-updates the copyright year via `new Date().getFullYear()` — no action needed there).
- [ ] Add/confirm a brand-usage note in legal/marketing materials ("StayKnit is a trademark of StayKnit (Pty) Ltd").
- [ ] Diarise the **renewal** (South African trademark registrations run for 10 years and are renewable; confirm the exact renewal date from the certificate and set reminders well ahead).
- [ ] Set up **brand watching** (self-monitoring or a paid watch service) to catch later confusingly-similar filings or infringing use.
- [ ] **Use it or risk it:** keep genuine, documented commercial use of the mark in each registered class (non-use over time can expose a mark to removal).
- [ ] Keep an evidence file of use (dated screenshots, invoices, marketing) in case you ever need to prove use or enforce.

## Phase 7 — Supporting brand assets (parallel, not strictly trademark)

- [ ] Confirm you own the key **domains** (stayknit.org and sensible variants/TLDs) and that they are locked/renewing.
- [ ] Secure matching **social media handles** where practical.
- [ ] Keep the **logo source files** and a short brand guideline (colours, spacing, misuse) with the company records.
- [ ] Ensure the company's IP (logo, code, content) is owned by StayKnit (Pty) Ltd via proper assignment from any contractors/designers.
